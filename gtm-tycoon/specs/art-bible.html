GTM Tycoon · Visual direction v1 · For the Claude Code build

Apollo, in 8 bits.

A pixel-art world that still feels like Apollo: warm, mostly monochrome, with one switched-on yellow that only lights up when something is good or active. The game and the brand share one rule: color is rationed, so the moments that matter glow.

01 · Two layers

The world is pixel. The desk is Apollo.

Every screen belongs to one of two layers. The split keeps the arcade fun and makes the "work" moments feel like the real product, which is where the brand shows up most.

WORLD LAYER

Map, arcade, battles, HUD, dialog. Pixel font, 8-color palette, hard edges, step animations, no smooth gradients.

Where: Inbox Plaza, Client District, gym, battles.

Desk layer

Memos, end-of-day results, upgrade choices, the persona directory. Editorial serif + clean sans on cream paper, like an Apollo document. Pixel art appears only as small inserts.

Where: the Office, results screens, title screen copy.

02 · Palette

Eight colors. One of them glows.

The six neutrals and indigo come from Apollo's public palette. Volt is Apollo's yellow-green accent. Signal is the only color added for the game, and it is reserved for mistakes.

Skin ramp (the one exception)

A strict palette would make every character the same tone. Characters use one of three warm skin tones so the cast reads as diverse.

04 · Cast

Original characters, built from a 24 × 30 grid.

Sprites are generated by code from a grid, so Claude Code can add new people without an artist. Buyer types are shown with a symbol badge plus a word, never by color alone.

05 · Lead icons

Readable in half a second.

Used in the arcade (Pipeline Panic) and on the map. Good leads are always the volt bag; everything else is drawn in neutrals. The icon's size is the company's size.

06 · Platform powers

The product shows up as power-ups.

Apollo's features are the items you find and the cards you pick. They are drawn as generic pixel icons for now. Once Apollo approves using the brand, they switch to the real symbol with a single flag (BRAND_MODE).

07 · The map

One screen. A whole workday.

A warm, monochrome city: no green grass, no tall grass. Streets are the "pipeline", lit windows are volt, and the leads you caught in the morning appear as people in the Client District. Tiles are 16 px, drawn ×2.

Volt: things you can use now Indigo: the Office (your HQ) Graphite: buildings, trees Limestone: streets Signal: only errors

10 · Rules

Do and don't.

Do

  • Use volt only for "good", "active" or "ready". If everything glows, nothing does.
  • Use signal only for mistakes and errors.
  • Pair every color cue with a shape or a word (buyer badges, ✓/✗ marks).
  • Use flat color and 4 px hard drop shadows. Animate in steps.
  • Keep arcade text to 3 words; put explanations in dialog or the desk layer.
  • Respect reduced motion: no shake or flash when it is on.

Don't

  • No Pokémon names, creatures, balls, tall grass, sounds or exact screen layouts. We borrow the genre, never the assets.
  • No Apollo logo, star symbol or product screenshots until Apollo approves. Until then, say "the platform".
  • No gradients, soft shadows, or cool blue-grays: the palette stays warm.
  • No real customer or prospect data. All companies and people are fictional.
  • No green grass or nature palette: the city is warm monochrome.
